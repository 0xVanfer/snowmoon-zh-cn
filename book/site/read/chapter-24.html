Chapter 24

帕佛盖都，泽国 · 3724年雾期27日

Pafogai Du, Dzego · 3724 Mistime 27

格拉迪亚斯看了一眼手表。时间刚跳到20,000。和塞拉通完电话后，他一整夜都没睡。他试了两次去睡，每次都撑了20分钟就放弃了。

他睡不着。4个他深爱的孩子里，已经有3个以这样那样的方式落进了北极人手里——他知道这一点。

他也睡不着。塞拉投了他一张不信任票，切断联系，连让他道歉的机会都不给。

他更睡不着。两件事同时压在他身上，能让他抱一抱、讨点安慰的人又在几千公里之外。

最后，他既想着女儿的安全，也想着在塞拉眼里挽回自己，于是决定做一件不合常规的事：他打给了兹文。

兹文立刻就接了。

“爸？”

“你还在和莉莉联系吗？”

“她的头像40分钟前变绿了。我想她是用新设备登录的。”

“好。”

“兹文，你愿意帮我们把莉莉救回来吗？”

“当然！为了我妹妹，什么都行。”

“那接下来，我要你做这么几件事。跟她保持联系。主动找她，问问她最近怎么样。别流露出半点不赞同她决定的意思，甚至要表现出同情。总之保持联系，想办法弄清她在哪儿。”

“爸……”

“怎么了？”

“这简直……跟妈妈一个长时前让我做的一模一样。她还教了我很多该说和不该说的话。”

格拉迪亚斯打开门，穿过走廊，走出酒店区域，然后左转，继续沿隧道往前走。

到今天，他住在地下已经整整8天，就住在帕佛盖都南端的山体金字塔里。他刚到泽国不久，就被安排从原先的酒店搬到了这里。新出台的建议是让所有“重要事务”一律转入地下，而格拉迪亚斯作为事实上的昆高培顾问，当然够格。

两个多月来，他每天都沿着山体金字塔半山腰的环山树林散步20分钟，有时还会往下走得更远，进到附近的森林里。最近这些天，他白天都待在图书馆，了解泽国的方方面面，几乎每天都和韦尔多通话，琢磨怎么说服维里迪亚军方与昆高培合作。

而今天，经历了昨晚那些事，又只睡了一个长时，他连出门的力气都提不起来了。

格拉迪亚斯走得很慢，去图书馆的路上常常停下，盯着墙面，或是打量经过的侧边房间。

他注意到，自他搬进来以后，各类高科技产业——生物医药、芯片制造，甚至汽车生产的一部分——都迁进了山体金字塔。再一琢磨，自从北林被吞并之后，新闻里就再没出现过一起北极对泽国的成功袭击。

他还注意到，散落各处的空气过滤器和紫外线灯多了10倍以上。至少泽国的厂商和班孙培干得不错，他想。

他经过一间食堂的入口，看见里面多了几棵树，种在陶盆里，约两米高，还摆着各色灌木。这些绿植似乎身兼三职：让坐在里面的人看着绿意心生平静，降低 CO2 浓度，还把食堂的不同区域隔开，多一分私密。

慢吞吞走了大约15分钟，他终于来到图书馆门口。

他发现门楣上的标语和上次来时不一样了。

sun jan li shai fe to dzan dzie ka jie dau fe sei sui ji cen hai zun
ci sun gau jan li gei shai fe to dzan dzie de mia ka ti dzie ka pu she

他停下脚步，花了一会儿工夫读那行字。

“学生说：明天，我想去一个神奇而遥远的地方。老师答道：为了明天，你今天做了什么？”

让格拉迪亚斯意外的是，这句训诫给了他一点力气，让他能抬手贴上绿色圆圈开门，走进图书馆。

他穿过门，慢慢向右转，朝其中一台计算机终端走去。

白注意到了他，走上前来。

“怎么了？”她问。

“我没事”，格拉迪亚斯用低沉而平淡的语气答道。

“不过，我了解得够多，看得出你心里并不好受。我也知道，如果你不情愿，还逼你说出心里那片阴影的来由，只会让你更难受。”

“所以别担心，我什么都不会问。”

她走到格拉迪亚斯面前，轻轻抱住了他。

“我刚刚才得知，莉莉这一年来对北极的宣传全盘接受，而埃菲里昂勋爵的布道让她觉得自己被重视、被欣赏，比我给她的多得多。不到一分钟，我又得知她离家出走了。又过一分钟，我得知塞拉现在恨我，不想跟我说话。”

“北极人说的那些话里，有哪些能让像她这样的人觉得有说服力？”

“无条件的爱毫无意义。爱只有在你爱对方身上某种品质时才值钱。所以要得到爱，你就得配得上它，得去挣。爱不能像恒定的背景辐射那样白白照着你，只能是赢来的奖赏。”

“最重要的是永远努力提升自己。可如果你能提升，就说明你能比过去的自己更好——那你就等于承认了，一个人可以比另一个人更好。所以，说什么我们人人平等、说什么我们身上最重要的是那份平等的人性——全是胡说。”

“你知道……我其实有点同意。”

“什么？”

“我不是说在什么绝对意义上，这种想法对每个人都健康。绝对不是。要是你真觉得它健康，那当然，一旦你认定自己最强，就会想去征服所有人，只为了证明这一点。我的意思是……这种想法对我有好处。”

“你是什么意思？”

“我父母在我很小的时候就死了。一场爆炸，北极人引发的。我跟着姨妈长大，她基本上不管我。没有人无条件地爱我。我12岁就得自己想办法挣钱。我的社交能力全是从那段经历里学来的。我从别人那里得到的唯一欣赏，就是在我给他们提供了有价值的东西、或者用成功让他们佩服的时候。你说的埃菲里昂那套循环——爱是赢来的奖赏——那就是我的童年。也正因为这样，我在帕佛盖都民本棋赛上拿了第二名。”

“第一名是谁？”

“泽。而他要赢的动机，比我那种要向世界证明‘我因为强，所以值得被爱’的求胜心，还要更纯粹、更漂亮。”

“那是什么动机？”

“好奇。”

对话停了一会儿。然后格拉迪亚斯又开口了。

“几分钟前你抱我的时候，你给的难道不正是埃菲里昂说的那种毒药吗？把爱当作对软弱的奖赏？”

“一年前我不会那么做。”

“可它管用了。”

“我很想说，我那么做是因为我抛弃了贪婪这个假神，改信了真正的宗教——看重好奇。可连这也是假的。如果我是出于好奇，我大概会直接问你出了什么事，而那只会惹你反感。”

“那倒是肯定的。”

“所以你的结论是什么？”

“也许真的没有什么万能公式。不同的人适用不同的招数。我不会说我对人类可能有的各种动机全都无所谓——我今天可以很坦然地说，好奇几乎总是比贪婪更好的动机。而怨恨可能是最糟的。但我不认为有哪一套能适合所有人。”

“那我要怎么救莉莉？还有，费布里克和赫雷达呢？还有，我怎么才能让塞拉不再恨我？”

“嗯，也许这正是你唯一需要变得至少有10%到20%像北极人的地方。如果我们能赢，你的家人就能完完整整地重聚。你从塞拉的角度想一想。她唯一能快乐的办法，就是能再抱一抱费布里克、赫雷达和莉莉。可光靠情感支撑和爱，没有任何办法把这3个人带回来。唯一能把他们带回来的，就是你赢——帮维里迪亚战胜北极人。所以，让这件事推着你更拼命一点，好让你在下一场战斗里成为赢家。”

格拉迪亚斯和泽沿隧道往下走，又下了楼梯。几分钟后，他们来到一扇密封的门前。泽把手表贴上门边的绿色圆圈。门缓缓打开了。

他们看见一个狭长的房间，房间尽头有一张大桌子。桌边坐着一个男人，披着一件暗影般的斗篷。

“欢迎你们两位！”登高声说。

“很高兴再见到你，登。”泽答道。

格拉迪亚斯清了清嗓子。“mi cin pin fe lo kin do”，他补了一句。

“哇，你的口音现在都这么好了。我也很高兴见到你，格拉迪亚斯。”

“对了，请坐。”

格拉迪亚斯和泽一起上前，在登对面的两把椅子上坐下。

“那么，格拉迪亚斯。这次会面是你召集的。你想谈什么？”

“嗯，到目前为止，我们还没想出什么好办法，能说服维里迪亚军方与你们合作。”

“不过，我倒有个不太常规的想法，可以让你们在过渡期里先做起来。”

“什么想法？”

“监视我们。”

登有些意外地看了格拉迪亚斯一眼，几乎立刻又恢复了平静，仿佛这才想起：公开流露兴奋或其他计划外的情绪，与他的自我形象不符。

“我想我能看出其中的道理。不过我想听你说。”

“北极人对维里迪亚社会的渗透非常深。他们腐蚀了许多政客，也腐蚀了军方。似乎只有那些内建隐私保护更强的机构还在抵抗：掌舵会、图谱资助和合议庭。不过即便在掌舵会内部，渗透也已经钻得很深。”

“所以，如果你们能监视维里迪亚，也许就能监视那些间谍。尽可能查清北极人对维里迪亚的渗透落在哪里，甚至试着反制。也许还可以把这些信息更直接地交到人们手里，让他们不必经过中央机构就能自己去解决。”

“你知道风险吧？”

“如果你们被抓住，维里迪亚和泽国之间的信任会大打折扣。那样我们就彻底分裂，北极人赢了。”

“那我们凭什么该接受这个风险？”

“嗯，首先，这局棋我们已经下到很后面了，局面很绝望。用你或者泽的话说，第九次干预回合。”

“第二，我不是只丢给你们一句‘去当间谍’就完了。我愿意提供关于北极人如何运作的许多具体细节，也能帮你们搭上我们最近联系上的一批掌则人，好让你们能更好地追踪北极人。”

“第三，我觉得我们应该告诉韦尔多，说服他和我一起担起责任：万一这事泄露出去，由我们两个人出面认下，说项目是我们提的，甚至是我们主持的。”

“我喜欢这个方案。泽？”

“你在问我？”

“当然。你已经是我们最好的密码学家之一，还一直在协调与自由城防卫队的合作。你觉得呢？”

“十五年来，数字设备上的安全一直非常牢固。间谍活动主要是物理世界的事，而物理世界要守住难得多。我觉得应该带格拉迪亚斯去看看一些店铺和晶圆厂，比如你很久以前带我看过的那家，然后我们可以一起想，有什么不太惹眼的办法能弄到更多关于维里迪亚的情报。”

“其实……我记得维里迪亚的监控摄像头是从泽国买的。就是已经装在不少地方、他们还想装到所有地方的那种。维里迪亚过去要求，摄像头装在公共场所时，必须用密码学手段强制执行数据用途限制，但几年前这一条被稀释了，是交通部一次没人注意的行政调整。你们能不能就……给那些摄像头留个后门？”

“如果真有这么容易，那北极人肯定早就大规模渗透维里迪亚了吧？”

“是啊，怎么了？”

“已经渗透了吗？”

“是啊，这一直就是我想告诉你们的啊！”

Gladias checked his watch. The time just hit 20,000. He had been awake all night after his call with Seila. He had tried to go to sleep twice, but gave up after twenty minutes each time.

He could not go to sleep knowing that three of the four children he loved dearly were now, in one way or another, in the hands of the Arctics.

He could not go to sleep after Seila gave him that kind of vote of non-confidence, and then cut off communication without giving him any chance to even apologize.

And he definitely could not go to sleep when both of those things were happening to him at the same time, and anyone he could hug for emotional support was thousands of kilometers away.

Finally, thinking as much about redeeming himself in Seila's eyes as about his daughter's safety, he decided to do something unorthodox: he called Zven.

Zven picked up immediately.

"Dad?"

"Are you still in contact with Lily?"

"Her icon went green about forty minutes ago. I think she logged in from a new device."

"Good."

"Zven, are you willing to help us save Lily?"

"Of course! Anything for my sister."

"Then here's what I want you to do. Stay in touch with her. Reach out, ask her what's up. Don't give any indication that you disapprove of her decision. Even show sympathy for her. Just stay in contact, and try to learn where she is."

"Dad..."

"What is it?"

"That's like ... exactly what Mom told me to do a longhour ago. She gave me a lot of good advice for things I should and shouldn't say."

Gladias opened the door, walked through the hallways until he left the hotel area, and then turned left and kept going through the tunnel.

This was now his eighth day living entirely underground, inside the mountain pyramid at the south end of Pafogai Du. Soon after he first arrived in Dzego, he had been relocated there from his original hotel. The new recommendations were for all "important business" to move to underground locations, and as a de-facto Kungaupei advisor, Gladias certainly qualified.

For over two months, he would take daily twenty minute walks every day along the trees around the perimeter halfway up the mountain pyramid, and sometimes a longer walk down and into the nearby forest. For the past few days, he had been spending each day in the library, learning about different aspects of Dzego and having almost daily calls with Verdow to think through how to convince the Veridian military to cooperate with the Kungaupei.

And today, after what happened last night, and after only getting one longhour of sleep after it, he did not have the will to go outside at all.

Gladias walked slowly, taking many breaks to stare at the wall and at any side rooms that he walked past on his way to the library.

High-tech industry of all kinds - biomedical, chip manufacturing, even parts of car production - had moved into the mountain pyramid since he came in, he noticed. And now that he thought about it, there had not been a single successful Arctic attack on Dzego in the news since Northglade had been annexed.

He also noticed that the number of air filters and ultraviolet lamps scattered around various locations had increased more than tenfold. At least the Dzegojan manufacturers and the Bansunpei are doing a good job, he thought.

He passed by the entrance to a cafeteria. Trees about two meters tall growing inside clay pots were now inside, he saw, along with various shrubs. The greenery seemed to play a triple role of giving the people sitting inside a feeling of calm from seeing nature, reducing CO2 levels, and splitting up different sections of the cafeteria to improve privacy.

After about fifteen minutes of slow walking, he finally arrived at the entrance to the library.

He noticed that the slogan at the top of the door had changed since the last time he had been there.

sun jan li shai fe to dzan dzie ka jie dau fe sei sui ji cen hai zun
ci sun gau jan li gei shai fe to dzan dzie de mia ka ti dzie ka pu she

He stopped, and took a moment to read.

"The student says: tomorrow I want to go to a magical and far away place. The teacher replies: for the sake of tomorrow, what have you done today?"

To Gladias's surprise, the admonishment gave him a small burst of energy, allowing him to raise his hand against the green circle to open the door, and enter the library.

He walked through the door, and then slowly turned right and started walking toward one of the computer terminals.

Bai noticed him, and walked up.

"What's the matter?", she asked.

"I'm fine", Gladias replied in a quiet and flat tone.

"Well, I know enough to know that you're not fine. Though I also know enough to know that forcing you to divulge the source of your darkness if you're not comfortable will make you feel even worse."

"So don't worry, I won't ask any questions."

She walked up to Gladias, and gently hugged him.

"I just learned that Lily has been eating up Arctic propaganda for the last year, and Lord Ephelion's sermons made her feel valued and appreciated more than I ever did. And less than a minute later, I learned that she ran away from home. And a minute after that, I learned that Seila now hates me and doesn't want to talk to me."

"What do the Arctics say that someone like her would find compelling?"

"That unconditional love is meaningless. Love is only worth anything if you love some quality that the object of your love has. And so to receive love, you have to deserve it, you have to earn it. Love has to come not as a constant background radiation, but as a reward for winning."

"The most important thing is to always strive to improve yourself. But if you can improve, that means you can be better than your former self - and at that point, you've basically admitted that one person can be better than another. So the whole idea that we're all equal, that the most important thing about us is our equal humanity, is total nonsense."

"You know ... I kind of agree."

"What?"

"I don't mean in some absolute sense, that that way of thinking is healthy for everyone. It definitely isn't. If you think it does, then of course once you think you're the best, you're going to want to start conquering everyone just to prove it. I meant more ... that that kind of thinking was good for me."

"What do you mean?"

"My parents died when I was very young. In an explosion, triggered by the Arctics. I grew up with my aunt, who basically just ignored me. No one loved me unconditionally. I had to start figuring out how to earn money on my own when I was twelve. I learned all my social skills from that. The only appreciation from other human beings that I got, was when I provided something of value to them, or when I impressed them with my success. The loop that you said Ephelion talks about, of love as a reward for winning, that was my childhood. And, I got second place in the Pafogai Du Minpentai games because of that."

"Who got first place?"

"Zei. And he had a motivation to succeed that was even more pure, even more beautiful, than my desire to succeed in order to prove to the world that I deserve love because I am strong."

"And what motivation was that?"

"Curiosity."

The conversation paused for a moment. Then Gladias spoke up again.

"When you hugged me a few minutes ago, were you not offering exactly the thing that Ephelion says is poison? Love as a reward for weakness?"

"I would not have done that a year ago."

"And yet it worked."

"I would love to say that I did that because I gave up the false god of greed and converted to the true religion, that of valuing curiosity. But even that is false. If I was motivated by curiosity, I would probably have just directly asked you what's wrong, and that would have triggered your resentment."

"It definitely would have."

"So what's your conclusion?"

"That maybe there really is no master formula. There are different bags of tricks that work for different people. I'm not going to say I'm fully unopinionated between all the different motivations that human beings might have - I'm very comfortable saying, today, that curiosity is almost always a better motivation than greed. And resentment might be the worst of all. But I don't think there's a one size fits all for everyone."

"So how do I save Lily? And, for that matter, Febric and Hreda? And how do I get Seila to stop hating me?"

"Well, maybe that's the one place where you do need to become at least ten or twenty percent Arctic. Your family will be back together, fully, if we can win. Think about it from Seila's perspective. The only way she can be happy, is if she can hug Febric, Hreda and Lily again. And there is no way to use emotional support and love alone to bring the three of them back. The only thing that will bring them back, is if you win - by helping Veridia win against the Arctics. So let that motivate you to try harder, so that in this next battle you can be a winner."

Gladias and Zei walked down the tunnel, and down the stairs. After a few minutes, they got to a sealed door. Zei tapped his watch against the green circle beside the door. The door slowly opened.

They saw a long room, and at the end of the room there was a large table. At the table, a man was sitting wearing a shadowy cloak.

"Welcome to you both!", Den exclaimed.

"Good to see you again, Den", Zei replied.

Gladias cleared his throat. "mi cin pin fe lo kin do", he added.

"Wow, even your accent is good now. And I'm happy to see you too, Gladias."

"By the way, please sit down."

Gladias and Zei both stepped forward, and sat down on two chairs opposite Den.

"So, Gladias. You called this meeting. What did you want to talk about?"

"Well, so far we have not figured out any amazing plan for how to convince the Veridian military to cooperate with you."

"But, I have a somewhat unorthodox idea for what you can do in the interim."

"And what is that?"

"Spy on us."

Den briefly stared at Gladias in surprise. His face almost immediately smoothed, as though remembering that it was not compatible with his self-image to be openly expressing any excitement or other unplanned emotion.

"I think I see the reasoning. But I want to hear yours."

"The Arctics have made a very deep infiltration into Veridian society. They've corrupted many of the politicians. They've corrupted the military. The only institutions that seem to be resisting are the ones that have some stronger privacy built in: the Order of Steering, Graph Funding and the Courts. Though even within the Order, the intrusion got in pretty deep."

"So if you can spy on Veridia, you might be able to spy on the spies. Figure out as much as you can about where the intrusion into Veridia is, and perhaps even try to counteract it. Maybe even put that information more directly into people's hands, and empower them to try to solve it without going through the central institutions."

"You know the risk, right?"

"If you get caught, a lot of trust between Veridia and Dzego is lost. And then we're divided completely and the Arctics win."

"So why should we be okay with that risk?"

"Well, first of all, we're already pretty late in the game and this is a desperate situation. A ninth intervention turn, as you or Zei might put it."

"Second, I'm not just giving you the one-word suggestion 'spy'. I'm offering to give some very detailed specifics about how the Arctics operate, as well as connections to a whole bunch of Keepers that we're now in contact with, so you can better track the Arctics down."

"Third, I think we should tell Verdow, and convince him to take responsibility, along with me, for proposing and even running the project if this all gets out."

"I like this plan. Zei?"

"You're asking me?"

"Of course. You're already one of our best cryptographers. You've been coordinating the work with the Freetown corps. What do you think?"

"For the past fifteen years now, security on digital devices has been very strong. Spying is primarily a physical-world thing, the physical world is much harder to secure. I think we should take Gladias on a tour of some of the stores and the fabs, like the one you showed me way back then, and we can try to think, what would be a reasonably unobtrusive way to get more information on Veridia."

"Actually ... I think Veridia buys its security cameras from Dzego. The ones that are already in a bunch of places, and that they wanted to put everywhere else too. Veridia used to require cryptographically enforced limits on what the data gets used for if the cameras are in public places, but that got watered down a few years back, an administrative change by the Transportation Ministry no one paid attention to. Could you just ... backdoor those?"

"If it was that easy, then surely the Arctics must have massively infiltrated Veridia already?"

"Yes?"

"Have they?"

"Yes? That's what I've been trying to tell you all along!"
