Chapter 23

帕佛盖都，泽国 · 3724年雾期22日

Pafogai Du, Dzego · 3724 Mistime 22

格拉迪亚斯、白和芬坐在山体金字塔深处一间食堂的桌旁。

食堂的桌椅与墙壁之间，四面都种着绿树。两面墙上爬满藤蔓，另外两面则镶着巨大的镜子，让整个房间仿佛置身于某种无限重复、无边无际的空间里。入口只有一个，位于一角，两侧各有一丛茂密的灌木，挡住了通往入口隧道的视线。

另一个角落里，以及离他们几米远的食堂正中，地上立着几台空气过滤器——显然是刚添置的，还没怎么考虑过怎样让它们在视觉上融入这个房间。天花板上吊着朝下的风扇和紫色的灯。

三人待在一处私密的隔间里，灌木挡住了食堂其余大部分区域的视线。两只扬声器模拟着清晨林中蝉鸣的声音，盖过了其他桌的谈话——他们知道，这同样也保护了他们自己的谈话。

泽走了进来，看得出已经精疲力竭，在他们旁边坐下。

“好险。我们遭到几架北极无人机的伏击，勉强才打赢。好在这波蜂群赶来把我们剩下的无人机清掉之前，我们还有整整五分钟，所以我想芯片都毁掉了，北极人没法从这场战斗里提取训练数据。”

“自由城那边的人情绪怎么样？”

“他们特别兴奋。”

“看吧，我早说过，先跟自由城合作，是让昆高培更愿意参与国际合作的好办法。”

“你得先给人一些能马上亮出来的成绩，让他们能拿给上级或朋友看，尽快靠实绩挣到正当性，别人才放心让你走下一步。”

“对，你的建议很管用。不过……我猜下一步会比这一步难。自由城防卫队愿意合作。到了维里迪亚，我们得想办法绕开他们的制度，那要难得多。”

“绕过人做点小项目，可以。但要想赢下大仗，就得有规模。而想要规模，就得做那件难事：真正把一些更大的机构拉到你这边来。”

“你有什么办法吗？”

“塞拉说她打算想想办法。我把韦尔多的联系方式给了她，他是我目前真正信得过的唯一一位参议员。后面再看吧。”

一个机器人过来，把四人的饭菜放上桌。他们安静地吃了起来。

芬和格拉迪亚斯先吃完，喝着茶歇了几分钟。

芬从包里取出一本书，递给格拉迪亚斯。

“来，看看你的泽国语学得怎么样。随便翻一页，念给我听。”

格拉迪亚斯接过书，随手翻到一页，看了一眼。

“你觉得我能读这个？”格拉迪亚斯不敢相信地问。

“来嘛，试试看。”

“好，那我开始了……”

“从物质世界的‘小思考’，到网络的‘小思考’。”

“‘小思考’就是极简主义。”

“啊，我明白了，这就是那种光知道词根也没用的例子。”

“确实。而且缩词根这件事，也是等我们承认它不可避免之后，才算做成的。”

“包在绿色里面——四十年前，全世界‘美的思考’的一次大模式变化，就是极简主义。”

“开头那一截是固定说法，意思是‘包括泽国和维里迪亚’。‘美的思考’就是美学。”

“许多人认识到，拥有许多实物不只是‘线心’的问题，还是一间‘锁住的房间’。”

“‘线心’是一种简朴、或者说方便的感觉；在这个语境里，‘锁住的房间’更像是监狱。”

“房间复杂，心也复杂。”

“好！”

“带着因东西少而来的‘暖心’——幸福？——活着，就是自由。”

“很好！”

“这件事发生在四十年前，是有逻辑的——有原因的？”

“对。”

“电子机器和互联网开始出现。”

“这意味着许多人攒下了实物……等等，是说被众人攒起来的那些实物，开始变得没人想要了。”

“这里我会说‘不必要’。”

“手持终端让‘聚合与分组装置’、音乐装置、声音交谈装置，以及以前每个人都会有的另外十种装置，都变得不必要了。”

“‘聚合与分组装置’应该就是加法与乘法装置——计算器。‘声音交谈装置’是电话。”

“它装书，可以没有上盖。”

“‘无限’。”

“我们大家生活中的许多部分，正在搬到电的世界里去。”

“是我们全部生活中的许多部分。”

“结果，物质世界的复杂性就停在了后面。”

“是被落在了后面。”

“在近旁的这几年里，我们正在寻求另一种模式变化。”

格拉迪亚斯顿了一会儿。

“好吧，我累了。但我好奇。你能不能直接告诉我，剩下的都是什么意思？”

泽拿起那本书。

“社交媒体并不总是对人的心理健康有好处。人们对隐私的关注，很大一部分不只是出于对北极人的恐惧，更因为所有人都在议论所有人，信息量大得铺天盖地，而且私生活里的偶然事件随时会变成公共话题，完全没法预料。”

“人们害怕互联网的许多部分，尤其是在隐私技术没这里强、也没这里这么普及的其他国家。位置追踪、行为画像，这一切。而且不只是隐私——人们正在意识到，互联网可以像毒品一样让人上瘾。”

“所以现在，我们慢慢看到另一种极简主义出现：网络极简主义。断开连接。只在自己的设备上做事。物质极简主义能成立，靠的是数字世界能力的增长；网络极简主义也一样，靠的是 AI 和本地软件能力的增长——你可以把整个世界装在自己的设备里。”

“有意思。”

“格拉迪亚斯，你读得很好。”

“谢谢。现在轮到你了。你对掌舵会的治理规则了解多少？”

“有一套税则评则。每一条评则随机抽二十一名掌则人——每年新增七名，任期三年。这个组的职责是提出并表决对评则的修改。二十一人分成三个七人小组，每组都混有第一年、第二年和第三年的人。任何掌则人都可以就修改评则提出提案。每个小组独立讨论每一项提案，彼此不知道对方是谁。讨论几轮之后，二十一名掌则人对每项提案投票，拿到二十一人中至少十三票赞成的，就算通过。”

“还有另一套机制，监审官。他们随机抽选，编成小组去审计单个企业。那边是三个三人小组。他们的任务很具体：判定一家企业在每条评则上落在哪一档。至于更小的企业，审计由见习官——也就是受训中的掌舵会成员——来做。见习官要被接纳，既要通过参议院委员会的批准，还要在预测分数上排进前10%——他们的审计有一小部分会被监审官随机抽样重做，而他们的表决结果与监审官结果的一致程度，就得排进前10%。”

“那合议庭呢？”

“机制和监审官一样，只是换成五名法官，而不是九名。另外，民事案件双方都可以上诉，刑事案件只有辩方可以。没有层级，只是再抽一个随机样本。还有一条普通法规则：如果你提交的案件——无论是上诉，还是第一次起诉——毫无道理到了恶意滥诉的程度，那这个案子最后不仅会判你输，还可能判你赔偿。这就是针对我们所说的‘拒绝服务攻击’的防护。”

“其实，你提到最后那点正好。维尔是我共同家庭里的另一位父亲，他打了一场很长的官司，告的……嗯，基本就是他以前的雇主，因为他们搞了些财务上的把戏把他解雇，好赖掉事先约定好的遣散费。第一轮他赢了。公司上诉，输了。然后他们又上诉了一次。就在昨天，法庭驳回了他们的第二次上诉，判定为恶意，并把他们要付给维尔和其他工人的金额提到了三倍。”

“很高兴听到这套制度管用！”

“最重要的问题永远是它到底管不管用。比如议会那套批准新掌则人、新监审官的听证程序，到目前基本上已经形同虚设了——至少在我看来是这样。”

白开口了。

“这些信息真的很有用，格拉迪亚斯；也谢谢你，泽，这么快就跟上了。如果我们想影响维里迪亚，搞清这些机构今天到底怎么运转，和军事那一面同样重要。”

“总之，登应该在他的私人房间等着我和泽了。我们该动身了。”

“好，”泽答道。

泽和格拉迪亚斯同时抢着把手表往桌角的绿色圆圈上贴，结果两人把对方的手腕都推得太远，谁也没连上。芬也凑上来，啪地把手表拍在圆圈上。片刻之后，他的手表震了一下。

四个人都站起身，往食堂外走。

格拉迪亚斯已经上床，正准备睡觉。突然，他的手表震了。

Gladias, Bai and Fin were sitting at a table in a cafeteria, deep underground inside the mountain pyramid.

Between the tables and the wall of the cafeteria, there were green trees on all sides. Two of the walls were covered with vines. The other two walls were covered with large mirrors, making the whole room seem like it was part of some kind of endlessly-repeating infinitely large space. There was a single entrance, with two thick shrubs on both sides blocking the view to the entrance tunnel, in one of the corners.

In one of the other corners, and in the middle of the cafeteria a few meters away from them, a few air filters stood on the ground - clearly recently added, with not yet much thought put in to how to make them fit into the room aesthetically. Downward-pointed fans and violet lights hung on the ceiling.

The three of them were in a private area, with shrubs blocking most of the rest of the cafeteria from view. Two speakers were simulating the sound of cicadas buzzing in a forest in the morning, drowning out the conversations from the other tables - and thus, they knew, protecting their own conversation as well.

Zei walked in, visibly exhausted, and sat down with them.

"That was close. We got ambushed by some Arctic drones, we just barely managed to beat them. Fortunately, we had a full five minutes before the next swarm came to finish ours off, so I think we were able to destroy any chips that the Arctics could extract to get training data from the battle."

"How are the Freetown folks feeling?"

"They're very excited."

"See, I told you that working with Freetown first is going to be a good way to warm the Kungaupei up to cooperating more internationally."

"You have to give people some quick wins that they can show their superiors or their friends, get legitimacy by performance as soon as possible so people trust you to take the next step."

"Yep, your advice was good. But ... I suspect the next step will be harder than this one. The Freetown defense corps want to cooperate. In Veridia, we'll have to find a way to work around their institutions, that's much harder."

"You can do small projects by working around people. But if you want to win the big battle, you need scale. And if you want scale, you have to do the hard thing, and actually bring some of the bigger institutions onto your side."

"Do you have any ideas on how?"

"Seila says she's going to try to figure something out. I gave her Verdow's contact, so far he's the only senator I really trust. So we'll see."

A robot arrived, putting food for the four of them onto the table. They began to quietly eat the food.

Fin and Gladias finished their food first, and rested for a few minutes, drinking their tea.

Fin took out a book from his bag, and handed it to Gladias.

"Here, let's see how good your Dzegoban is. Open up a page and read."

Gladias grabbed the book and flipped to a random page. He took a look.

After a few rounds of discussion, all twenty one Keepers vote on each of the proposals, and the ones that pass, with at least thirteen out of twenty one votes approving them, get accepted."

"There is a separate mechanism, the Sentinels, who are randomly selected and put into groups that audit individual businesses. There, it's three groups of three. They have a very specific task: determine what tier on each rubric a business gets. And then smaller businesses have audits done by Acolytes, Order members in training. For an Acolyte to get accepted, they have to both pass Senate committee approval, and be in the top ten percent by prediction score - by how well their votes on the small portion of their audits that get randomly sampled and re-done by Sentinels agree with the Sentinels' results."

"Now, what about the Courts?"

"Same mechanism as the Sentinels, though with five judges instead of nine. Also, in civil cases either side, in criminal cases only the defense, can appeal a ruling. There's no hierarchy, it just selects another random sample. There's a common law rule that if you submit a case - either an appeal, or even a first-time lawsuit - that has no merit to the point of being bad-faith, then that case can end not just with you losing, but even with a penalty judgement against you. That's the protection against what we call denial of service attacks."

"Actually, good that you mention that last part. My co-father, Vil, was in a long lawsuit against, well, basically his former employer, for doing some financial trickery to fire him and get away without paying the pre-agreed severance. He won the first round. The company appealed and lost. Then they appealed again. And just yesterday, the court struck down their second appeal, declared it bad faith, and tripled the amount that they have to pay Vil and the other workers."

"Glad to hear the system works!"

"That's always the most important question: whether or not it actually works. The Parliament hearings approving new Keepers and Sentinels, for example, barely work at all at this point - at least in my view."

Bai spoke up.

"This is all really good info, Gladias, and thank you Zei for getting up to speed on it so quickly. If we want to affect Veridia, understanding how these institutions all work today is just as important as the military side."

"Anyway, I think Den is waiting for me and Zei in his private room. We should start heading over."

"Sounds good", Zei replied.

Zei and Gladias simultaneously raced to put their watches against the small green circle on the corner of the table. They ended up both pushing each other's wrists too far away to make the connection. Fin joined in, and slapped his watch against the circle. A moment later, his watch buzzed.

The four of them all stood up, and began to walk to leave the cafeteria.

Gladias was in bed, getting ready to go to sleep. Suddenly, his watch buzzed.

FromMessageTime
Seila
→[requesting call]91454

He had only seen messages in this shade of red twice before: once when Seila had messaged him when Northglade was attacked, and once when she had called him about Delwart.

Emerald was letting Gladias know the level of importance and Seila's mood and, Gladias knew, this was already a large part of the message.

Gladias's heart raced, as he answered the call.

"DO YOU KNOW WHAT HAPPENED TO LILY?", Seila screamed.

"I ... do not. I just remember hearing that she got eighty-one percent on her latest exam. What happened?!"

"I entered her room. She was talking, typing on her hand device. And I could see what she was reading and saying in the mirror."

"For a few minutes, I just stared. I could not believe it."

"What was it?"

"She was in some kind of chat group, and they were sharing Arctic propaganda. Cheering on Lord Ephelion's messages. Hoping that the Arctics would come to the rest of Veridia soon."

"WHAT?"

"I snapped. I called her out on it, and asked how she could possibly be betraying us like this, when Febric and Hreda were stuck a thousand kilometers away because of them."

"What did she reply?"

"That the Arctics were right. Their philosophy, their idea that some people are worse and other people are better, and that the better people should rule everyone else, and the most important thing in life is to strive to be one of the better people, was the thing that had motivated her, and given her a reason to study and work hard. That our unconditional love was poison. That the Arctics' philosophy - appreciation and love only as reward for success - actually brought her out of her hole, when nothing we did helped even a little bit."

"That's ... horrible."

"I screamed at her for ten minutes, and shut the door."

"I cried for a longhour. And then, when I opened the door again, the window was open, and she was gone."

"Can you ... track her? Through her watch?"

"Already got Mov to do that. It was a waste of time, she threw her watch onto some random autobus."

"Wow. Now I feel so bad. We really haven't been doing anything to actually motivate or encourage her."

"GLADIAS, SHE GOT BRAINWASHED BY THE ARCTICS. DON'T YOU DARE GET IN YOUR PHILOSOPHER MOOD AND TELL ME THEY HAVE A POINT."

"HOW COULD YOU HAVE BEEN SO NEGLIGENT?"

"Okay, hold on. Not seeing through Delwart, that one is on me. I was dumb. Mov was dumb. I admit it. This, this is on both of us. You're Lily's mom. You saw her many times - before she went to Vil and Daia's, when we visited them during her time with them, and after she came back. You had many chances to make things better, just like I did. And you didn't succeed, just like I didn't succeed. So let's solve this problem together."

"YOU DON'T UNDERSTAND AT ALL, DO YOU?"

Seila hung up the call.
